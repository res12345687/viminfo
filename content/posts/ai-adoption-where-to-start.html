Most leadership teams no longer ask whether to use AI. They ask where to start without wasting money. The answer is less about technology than about choosing the right first problem.

Start with a problem, not a tool

"We need a chatbot" is a tool decision. "Our support team spends half its day answering the same twenty questions" is a problem worth solving. Begin by listing the tasks that eat the most staff hours, cause the most errors, or slow customers down the most. AI is a candidate for the ones that involve reading, writing, sorting or summarizing large volumes of text or data.

Good first use cases

  • Customer support: drafting replies to common queries for an agent to check and send.
  • Document processing: pulling key fields out of invoices, purchase orders, applications or contracts.
  • Internal knowledge search: letting staff ask questions of policies, SOPs and manuals in plain language.
  • Sales support: summarizing calls, drafting proposals and updating the CRM.
  • Reporting: turning raw numbers into a written weekly summary for management.

What these have in common: a human stays in the loop, mistakes are easy to catch, and the time saved is easy to measure.

Run a pilot, not a programme

Pick one use case, one team and a time box of six to eight weeks. Before you start, write down how you'll measure success: hours saved per week, turnaround time, error rate or customer satisfaction. Measure the current baseline first, or you won't be able to prove anything at the end.

Keep the pilot small enough that it can fail cheaply. A failed pilot that teaches you something is a good outcome. A year-long rollout that nobody uses is not.

Get the basics right

  • Data: know what data the tool will see, where it is stored and who can access it. Check your obligations under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act before sending customer data to any third-party service.
  • Review: decide who checks AI output before it reaches a customer, and what happens when it's wrong.
  • People: involve the team doing the work from day one. They know where the real friction is, and they'll decide whether the tool gets used.

Scale what works

When a pilot hits its targets, document what made it work, then extend it to the next team or the next use case. Over time, those small wins add up to a real AI capability rather than a collection of experiments.
