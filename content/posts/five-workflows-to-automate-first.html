In most organizations, skilled people spend hours every week copying data between spreadsheets, chasing approvals over email and rebuilding the same reports. Automation frees that time, cuts errors and makes work easier to track. These five workflows are usually the best place to start.

1. Invoice and bill processing

Invoices arrive by email, someone types them into the accounting system, someone else checks them against purchase orders, and approvals happen in a long email thread. Automation can read the invoice, match it to the order, route it to the right approver and post it once approved. Finance teams get faster closes and fewer missed payments.

2. Employee onboarding

A new joiner needs an offer letter, documents collected, a laptop, email and system accounts, and a payroll record. When each of those is a separate request, something always gets missed. A single onboarding workflow triggers every task the moment an offer is accepted and shows HR what's still pending.

3. Lead capture and follow-up

Enquiries come in through the website, WhatsApp, phone and email, and many of them are never followed up. Automation puts every lead into one CRM, assigns it to a salesperson, sends an instant acknowledgement and reminds the team when a lead goes quiet.

4. Recurring reports

If someone spends Monday morning pulling numbers from three systems into a spreadsheet, that report can be automated. Connect the data sources once and the dashboard updates itself, so managers see current figures instead of last week's.

5. Approvals

Leave requests, purchase requisitions, expense claims and discount approvals all follow a fixed set of rules. Put those rules into a workflow and requests go to the right person automatically, with reminders, escalation and a full audit trail. That trail is especially valuable for government and PSU teams.

How to pick your first one

  • High volume: it happens many times a week.
  • Rule-based: the steps are mostly the same each time.
  • Painful: it causes delays, errors or complaints today.
  • Measurable: you can count the time or errors before and after.

Map the process as it really runs, not as the policy document says it runs. Fix the obvious waste first, then automate. Automating a broken process only makes it break faster.
