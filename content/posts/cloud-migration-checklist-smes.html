Moving to the cloud can cut IT overheads, make remote work easier and remove the risk of a single server failing in the office. It can also go badly: surprise bills, broken integrations and staff who can't find their files. The difference is usually preparation. Here is the checklist we use with growing businesses.

1. Take stock of what you have

List every application, server, database and shared drive, who uses it and what it connects to. Include the "invisible" pieces: the Excel macro finance relies on, the scheduled backup script, the licence tied to one machine. You can't plan a move for systems you don't know about.

2. Decide what to do with each system

  • Replace it with a SaaS product (for example, moving email and files to a cloud office suite).
  • Move it as it is to cloud servers, which is fastest but saves the least.
  • Rebuild it to use cloud services properly, which costs more upfront but pays back over time.
  • Retire it if nobody really needs it any more.
  • Keep it on-premise if there's a genuine reason, such as specialized hardware or a regulatory requirement.

3. Work out the real cost

Compare what you spend today (hardware, power, maintenance, staff time, licences) against expected cloud costs. Cloud pricing is pay-as-you-go, so estimate usage honestly and set budget alerts from day one. Many cloud overruns come from resources that were switched on for a test and never switched off.

4. Plan security and compliance

  • Turn on multi-factor authentication for every account.
  • Give people access only to what their role needs.
  • Check where your data will be stored. Major providers offer data centres in India, which can matter for customers, regulators and government contracts.
  • Decide who owns security settings after the move.

5. Move in phases

Start with something low-risk, such as file storage or a non-critical internal app. Learn from it, then move the next system. Avoid a "big bang" weekend where everything changes at once.

6. Test your backups and your exit

Back up before every step, and actually restore a backup to prove it works. Also know how you'd get your data out if you ever changed provider.

7. Train people and switch off the old

Show staff where things live now and how to do their daily tasks. Then decommission the old systems on a set date. Running both in parallel indefinitely doubles your costs and confuses everyone.
